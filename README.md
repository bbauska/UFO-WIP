ufo work in progress.

March 4,1969. Atlanta, Missouri. About sunrise. William Overstreet, 50 years old, was driving in a rural area on hisway to work when he saw a bright reddish-orange light. As he drew nearer to the light, he realized it was a huge (estimated 100 feet in diameter) ball, which was floating along at about
40 mph and following the contours of the land. As it began
to cross the highway, it stopped and then began to follow the
road. Overstreet approached in his truck, and the ball beamed
a "strong white light" onto the road from its height of about
50 feet. The light beam was cone-shaped, ending at the road
surface in an 8-foot diameter spot. Heat radiating from the
beam had a similar effect on Overstreet of sitting in a closed
car on a hot sunny summer day. When the beam came on, the
object changed from red to a blue sphere surrounded by a red
ring, which surrounded a whirling yellow.

Overstreet, a postman, was obviously not going to let wind nor rain nor sleet
nor weird balls of light deter him from his appointed rounds, and he attempted
to drive through the light beam. When he got to within 
six feet, both his motor and radio died. The ball and its beam moved a little further
down the road, and the engine and radio were active again. He tried again to approach 
the beam, and the failures occurred just as before. This time he let the beam mosey a bit
further down the road and followed discreetly. The ball got about a mile away, turned back 
to red, switched off its beam, and then left the road. As it crossed the nearby power lines, 
it flashed and then cruised away until lost to sight. Then it was just another clear sunny 
morning in northern Missouri. The effect on Overstreet? "I had never believed in them too 
much until I saw this. Now I know that there is something to these UFOs. I don't know why it 
was there or where it was going. Neither do I know what it was after I saw it, but I don't 
care about being that close to another one." Missouri is the Show Me state.
